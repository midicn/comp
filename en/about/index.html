What this station is for

This is the people station. It follows the person as the thread that ties works, era and style together — because many musical questions open right up once you approach them from "who wrote this, for whom, and why in this way".

What it will contain

  • Composer profiles — life and era (roughly how the life went, and what the musical world around it was like), stylistic traits you can actually hear, and relationships with contemporaries.
  • Key works — a handful per composer, each with one line on what to listen for.
  • Playable examples — every work mentioned gets a version that really exists in the library, one click away.
  • Chinese composers — included where the copyright status is clear enough to quote and play the music publicly.

What each profile looks like

  • It opens with an identity: one line on why this person is worth remembering.
  • Then a timeline: birth, teachers, posts, major works, later years.
  • Then a few key works, each with one line on what to listen for.
  • In both Chinese and English, with names and dates written consistently in each.

How to read it

1. As a reference — hear something unfamiliar and want to know who wrote it. 2. As a gateway — start from a composer and move out to genres, instruments and periods. 3. As a playlist — read only the key works and listen straight through.

Where it stands today

Profiles are being added over time. This station depends on checking identities one by one — shared names, variant spellings, dates and attributions all have to be verified before anything is written — so it is being published in batches, starting with the people who are easiest to confuse. The inclusion criteria for Chinese composers (dates and copyright status) will be stated before the writing starts. :::
